What both programs share.
Both programs are designed and operated to the same standards — so a partner who joins one can adopt the other without re-learning the rules.
DATA
PLACEHOLDER
DATA
Shared data layer
Common data model for SMEs, cooperatives, parcels, harvests, transactions, waste flows, and emissions. Interoperable with Digital Earth Africa, FAO HiH, GIEWS, SERVIR and CGIAR GARDIAN.
ETHICS
PLACEHOLDER
ETHICS
Shared ethics & privacy
One AI Ethics policy. One data-protection regime — Rwanda Law 058/2021, Kenya DPA 2019, PIPEDA, GDPR where applicable. SMEs and cooperatives own their data.
MEAL
PLACEHOLDER
MEAL
Shared MEAL framework
One results framework, one indicator dictionary, one external mid-term evaluation, one learning agenda. Donors get a single, consistent reporting view across both programs.
GEDSI
PLACEHOLDER
GEDSI
Shared GEDSI & safeguarding
Same gender, disability and inclusion targets across both tracks. Same PSEAH, do-no-harm and grievance-redress mechanism in local languages.
How the two programs work together.
We track every dollar from inputs through activities to outcomes to impact. Below is the high-level view; the full diagrams sit on the Theory of change page.
Combined theory of change for PAGES and ICES, by layer
Layer	PAGES	ICES
Inputs	Grants, technical staff, McGill MMA capstone, RCID & SEKU faculty	Grants, technical staff, Terre des Jeunes & Ripen field teams
Activities & outputs	Three tools deployed, BDS training, cooperative onboarding, policy briefs	Six tools deployed, youth and women cooperatives onboarded, sorting infrastructure
Outcomes	Higher prices, lower post-harvest loss, finance access, climate-smart inputs	Higher waste capture, methane/CO₂ averted, livelihoods for youth and women
Impacts	Resilient agri-SMEs, food-secure households, formalised cooperatives	Cleaner municipalities, monetised circular flows, verified climate finance
Cross-cutting	GEDSI quotas met, AI & data ethics enforced, conflict sensitivity applied	Same — quotas, ethics, conflict sensitivity, do-no-harm
